# The Godwell — World Bible

> Canon core. Everything in-game (item flavour, notes, NPC lines, secrets)
> must agree with this page. Expanded sections below the line are written
> from this core.

## Canon core (fixed decisions)

**The Kneeling God.** Above the Godwell kneels the Colossus: a headless giant
of grey stone, hands cupped over the pit. The people of Kneel believe it is a
monument their ancestors carved to honour God, who went down into the earth.

**The truth (revealed only in fragments, never all at once):** the Colossus
*is* God's body. God grew weary of a world that only ever asked. It knelt at
the edge of the world, set its head down into the earth, and went to sleep —
to dream a world where, for once, *it* could be the one who asks.

**The Godwell is that dream, pressed into stone.** Each stratum is God
dreaming of a people who once prayed to it — each of which tried, in its own
way, to *reach* God, and went down, and was swallowed into the dream:

1. The Undercroft — those who tried to reach God through **death** (the first
   villagers of the rim; they buried themselves closer).
2. The Drowned Archive — through **knowledge** (scholars who tried to write
   God's true name; the ink ran, the water rose).
3. The Mycelial Choir — through **communion** (a people who dissolved into one
   singing mind so their prayer would be loud enough).
4. The Cinder Foundry — through **making** (smiths who tried to forge a god
   they could hold).
5. The Gilded Hive — through **service** (worshippers who became a hive,
   feeding a Queen they mistook for God).
6. The Frozen Liturgy — through **devotion** (a mass that swore never to end
   its prayer; time froze to keep the promise).
7. The Red Garden — through **flesh** (those who tried to grow a body worthy
   of godhood).
8. The Orrery — through **control** (engineers who built a machine to turn
   the dreamer in its sleep — it still turns).
9. The Unlit — through **denial** (those who put out every light so God would
   have to come looking for them).
10. The Threshold — the dreamer itself. It dreams of Kneel, remembered wrong.

The deeper you go, the older the people. Kneel is the eleventh rim-village.
Its founders are not remembered because the dream does not remember
beginnings.

**Why the floors change.** A dream is never the same twice. The Well "breathes"
(its tides reshape the floors) — procedural generation is diegetic.

**Why death takes your loot.** The dream keeps what it touches. A dead delver's
belongings fold into a **Reliquary** — a coffer the dream grows around them —
until the floor is forgotten.

**Why five floors.** "The Well does not let go of what it has not tasted." A
delver must go five breaths deep before the dream knows them well enough to
let them wake (Ascend). The **Waking Bell** rings the dreamer half-awake for a
moment — enough to slip out.

**Why gear decides depth.** "The Well takes you as deep as you are heavy."
The portal weighs what a delver carries — the deeds it remembers in each item
(ilvl). Old Hask's **Kits** are "light gear": weighed and borrowed, bound to
Hask by a candle.

**Other delvers.** Sometimes two dreamers' dreams overlap on the same floor.
You hear the other's heartbeat before you see their lantern.

**The Candle Order.** The quiet keepers of the Chandlery in Kneel light one
candle for every delver who goes down. They know more than they say: that the
dream must not wake, or must — the Order is split, and that split is the long
game of the setting.

**What waits at the bottom.** The Head. Eyes closed. Dreaming. What happens when
a delver reaches it is the game's final question and is *not* resolved in
text: God will ask *you* something.

## Tone

Weird, melancholy, dry-humoured, specific. No chosen ones, no prophecy, no
dark lords, no elves/dwarves/orcs. Prefer concrete objects over abstractions
("a spoon with teeth marks", not "an ancient evil"). Every stratum's people
had their own dignity and their own mistake.

## 1. The shape of things

### 1.1 The Postures

Every rim-village named itself for the way it stood before God. Nobody in
Kneel knows the full list. The strata give it up a piece at a time — each
people names its own village in its notes; the Orrery's star-charts name
three of their neighbours; one mirror in the Unlit has all of them scratched
into its back, in reverse. Oldest first:

| # | Village | Where it is now | How it reached |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Stand** | The Threshold (floors 91–100) | it didn't — it watched |
| 2 | **Turn** | The Unlit (81–90) | denial |
| 3 | **Reach** | The Orrery (71–80) | control |
| 4 | **Offer** | The Red Garden (61–70) | flesh |
| 5 | **Fold** | The Frozen Liturgy (51–60) | devotion |
| 6 | **Bend** | The Gilded Hive (41–50) | service |
| 7 | **Raise** | The Cinder Foundry (31–40) | making |
| 8 | **Lean** | The Mycelial Choir (21–30) | communion |
| 9 | **Stoop** | The Drowned Archive (11–20) | knowledge |
| 10 | **Bowe** | The Undercroft (1–10) | death |
| 11 | **Kneel** | the rim, for now | — |

Stand is not dreamed as a *people*. It is the last thing God saw before it
closed its eyes: a village at the edge of a hole, everyone standing, staring.
The dream fills that memory with whatever village stands at the rim now —
which is why the Threshold looks like Kneel, remembered wrong.

The list trends downward, from upright to prone, with detours. The word that
comes after *Kneel* is obvious. Nobody in Kneel says it. The Wakers write it
on walls; the Keepers paint over it.

All eleven villages stood on the same ground. There is only one rim. Each
people went *down* — dug, drowned, sank, sang, marched — and the next village
was built on its cellars. Kneel's cellars keep breaking through into older
cellars with the same floor plans. This is why the canon calls Bowe "the first
villagers of the rim": they are the first anyone in Kneel remembers, and they
stood exactly where Kneel stands.

### 1.2 The Dreamed Questions

In the dream, God is the one who asks. Each stratum is haunted by one
question, the question God asks the people it is dreaming. It is never shown
as a heading or a quest objective. It is carved on lintels, muttered by
creatures, scratched in graffiti, embedded in a warden's last words, and
answered — badly, endlessly — by the people of the stratum. A player who has
seen all ten should feel the shape of the eleventh.

| Stratum | The question | Why that one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Undercroft | *Where did you think you were going?* | Bowe believed the dead go down to God. God never knew where the dead go. |
| Drowned Archive | *What is my name?* | Stoop tried to write God's true name. God doesn't know it either. |
| Mycelial Choir | *Which of you is speaking?* | Lean became one voice. Nobody is left to answer. |
| Cinder Foundry | *Is it heavy?* | Raise tried to forge a god they could hold. |
| Gilded Hive | *What do you want?* | Bend served. Servants are not allowed to want. |
| Frozen Liturgy | *Are you finished?* | Fold swore never to stop praying. |
| Red Garden | *Is this what I look like?* | Offer grew a body for God to wear. |
| Orrery | *Where are you taking me?* | Reach built a machine to steer the dreamer. |
| Unlit | *Where are you?* | Turn hid in the dark so God would come looking. It is looking. |
| Threshold | — | The Head will ask the delver. See §5. |

Writers: the questions are asked kindly, curiously, a little clumsily — the
voice of something enormous that has only ever been asked things and is
trying the other side for the first time. Never sinister. Never booming.

## 2. Kneel

### 2.1 What Kneel says about itself

Ask anyone at the Stump and you get the same story, with the same pauses:

> Our great-great-however-many-grandparents came to the rim with a spade and
> a candle each. God had gone down into the earth — everybody knew that; you
> could still see the hole. So they carved the Giant, kneeling, so that God
> would look up one day and know somebody up here was still sorry. Took them a
> hundred years. They buried their dead in the crypts under the rim so they'd
> lie nearer to God, which is the Undercroft, and which is why we don't bury
> any more. Too many of them got up. The Candle Order was founded to keep a
> light for every soul that goes down. The Well breathes, and things come up
> on the breath — gold, bone, jars of strange oil — and people go down after
> them. Some come back rich. Nobody's come back from the bottom, which only
> goes to show how deep it is.

Sorry for *what*, nobody can tell you. "For asking so much," says Mother
Sallow, if pressed, and then changes the subject.

### 2.2 What the fragments say

None of these is ever stated outright. Each is a thing a player can see,
read or trip over.

* **The Giant has no tool marks.** Not one. Its lichen grows in rings like a
  tree's; a novice of the Order once counted the rings on one patch by the
  left elbow and gave up at four thousand.
* **Its knees go *into* the bedrock**, the way knees go into mud. Carved
  statues stand *on* things.
* **The neck is not broken.** It is smooth and rounded, like the end of a
  wrist. Set down, not knocked off.
* **Kneel has no year one.** Its calendar counts "this year", "last year",
  "the year of the goat", and then stops. The Chandlery's oldest ledger begins
  in the middle of a sentence: "…and so we lit one for her too."
* **The cellars.** Kneel's cellars break through into older cellars with the
  same floor plans, the same drains, the same place for the potatoes.
* **The surnames.** Quell, Rook, Sallow, Crane, Fenwright, Ferrow. They are
  on Kneel's doors, and they are in the strata, in hands centuries older than
  anyone. No one has an explanation. The Keepers say names are few and people
  are many. The Wakers say the dream is running out of names.
* **The fog is closing in.** The Market Ring had forty stalls within living
  memory. It has thirty-one. The Wakers keep a line of marker stakes on the
  south road; most years, one is lost. It is not stolen. It is just no longer
  anywhere.
* **The little finger.** An old drawing in the Chandlery shows the Giant's
  left hand with its little finger straight. It is not straight now.
* **Letters from below.** The Drowned Archive contains letters addressed to
  Kneel streets. One of them is addressed to a street that has not been built
  yet.

### 2.3 The lie of the land

Landmarks that level design, secrets and writing all refer to:

* **The Market Ring** — a circle of stalls around the **Godwell portal**: a
  ring of worn kneeling-stones around a disc of black, breathing air. On the
  ring: the **grain scale** (an iron beam scale on a gantry, weights marked in
  floors instead of pounds) and the **Founders' Plinth** (a stone plinth with
  nothing on it; "taken down for cleaning", everyone says, and has said for
  generations).
* **The Colossus** — kneels on the north rim, a hundred metres of grey stone,
  hands cupped over the pit. The left thumb rests on the rim — you can walk
  onto it ("the Thumb"). Its robes fall in stone folds as big as houses;
  behind the left knee is a dark crack everyone calls **the Robe Fold**.
* **The Bell Tower** — squat, east of the ring. A bronze bell with no
  clapper. Its lip has been filed bright all the way round.
* **The Chandlery** — the Candle Order's house. Long, low, windowless, warm
  as an oven. Thousands of candles on stepped shelves, one per delver, each
  with a name scratched in with a pin. On the top shelf, **the Long Candle**.
* **The Little Well** — the village's drinking well, in Well Lane, kept by
  Absalom Quell. Not the Godwell. It whispers.
* **The Memorial Wall** — the retaining wall along the rim path, chalked and
  scratched with the names of the delvers who did not come back.
* **The Delvers' Hall** — bunks, your stash chest, a noticeboard nobody
  reads, a stove everybody sits round.
* **The Stump** — the tavern, named after the Giant's neck. Salt on every
  table. A chair by the window nobody sits in.
* **The Ash Step** — a stone terrace on the south rim where Kneel burns its
  dead.
* **Tanner's Row** — a narrow lane of shut houses. Number thirteen has no
  handle on the outside.
* **The Rope Walk** — the path along the rim, with little cairns of pocket
  stones at every bend.
* **The Fog** — every edge. Walk into it and you walk out into the market,
  facing the Giant.
* Shops: **The Sallow Jar** (apothecary), **The Cinder Bench** (artificer),
  **The Hollow Coin** (wagers), **Pell & Needle** (tailor), **The Lending
  House** (quartermaster).

### 2.4 Customs

**The send-off.** A first descent goes like this, and every descent after is
a shortened version of it:

1. *The weighing.* The delver stands on the grain scale. The beam settles on
   a floor number. Everybody pretends it's a formality. It is the only honest
   measurement in Kneel.
2. *The candle.* The Chandler scratches the delver's name into a fresh candle
   with a pin and lights it from the Long Candle. It goes on the shelves. It
   will burn as long as the delver is alive and no longer.
3. *The pocket stone.* Somebody — a parent, a friend, a stranger if there's
   no one else — picks a pebble off the rim and puts it in the delver's
   pocket. "So you've something of ours to bring back."
4. *The rim words.* The sender says **"Kneel low."** The delver answers
   **"And rise after."**
5. *The turning.* Nobody watches a delver go in. The people at the rim turn
   their backs. It is rude to watch. Nobody knows why it is rude.

**The return.** A delver who comes back is sat down at the Stump and given
bread and a pinch of salt, and nobody may ask them anything until morning.
**"No questions till morning"** is the only law in Kneel that is never broken.
Children who break it are sent to bed; adults who break it are not served.

**The candles of the dead.** When a delver dies, their candle gutters on its
own, whatever the draught. Only the Chandler may pinch the wick. The stub is
never relit; it is melted down into the next new candle, "so nobody goes down
entirely alone". The Chandler knows before anyone. This is the second-oldest
tradition in Kneel. (The oldest is the rendering-vats of Bowe, and nobody in
Kneel has made that connection. Players will.)

**The dead of Kneel** are not buried. They are burned on the Ash Step and the
wind is let take them — "up, for once." Burial is considered bad manners, like
pushing in.

**The Longest Breath.** Once a year, when the Well's breathing is at its
lowest ebb, the whole village stands at the rim and holds its breath. The last
one to breathe gets to hit the clapperless bell with a stone. It makes a sound
like a pan dropped in another house.

**The counting.** Children sit on the rim steps and count delvers going down
and delvers coming up. The totals never match. Sometimes more come up.

**Dreams** are never told at breakfast. You may tell your dream after noon,
and then only to someone who is eating.

### 2.5 Sayings

* "Kneel low." / "And rise after." — farewell at the rim; also said when
  somebody's about to do something foolish.
* "No questions till morning." — leave them be.
* "Heavy as you are." — you get what you deserve.
* "It'll come out in the breathing." — this will change on its own; stop
  fussing.
* "Don't make the Well a promise." — don't swear to anything you can't keep.
* "Ask the Giant." — nobody knows; also, "mind your own business."
* "The Giant's got no head for sums." — said of a bad bookkeeper, a bad
  gambler, or the Order.
* "Bread's for the living, candles for the rest."
* "Lost as a Stoop letter." — hopelessly misdirected. (Nobody knows what a
  Stoop is. It's just what you say.)
* "Deep enough to drown a bishop." — very deep. (There have never been
  bishops in Kneel. Things in the Frozen Liturgy wear mitres.)
* "Don't answer it." — said to children who talk in their sleep.

### 2.6 The counting rhyme

Kneel's children use this to count out who's "it". Whoever lands on the last
word is *down the Well* and has to run to the fog and back. It lists the ten
strata in order. No adult has noticed, because no adult has been to all ten.

> One is a candle and one is a spade,
> Two is a letter the water unmade,
> Three is a song with no singer inside,
> Four is a hammer that tried and it tried,
> Five is a honeycomb, sticky and sweet,
> Six is a hymn with the snow on its feet,
> Seven's a garden that grows you a face,
> Eight is a clock that is turning the place,
> Nine is the dark, so hide, little one,
> Ten is back home, and the counting is done —
> *out goes you.*

Tibb knows two more lines, and will only sing them to a delver who has been
to the Threshold:

> Eleven is us, on our knees by the hole —
> Twelve is lying down, and it's having a rest.

(It doesn't rhyme. Tibb says it's not supposed to. "Twelve doesn't have to
rhyme, it's lying down.")

### 2.7 Residents

**Mother Sallow** — *The Sallow Jar.* Sixties, three aprons, hands stained
yellow to the wrist. Her husband Orrin went down forty years ago and she
still warms his side of the bed with a brick. Doses every returning delver
with something bitter "to get the dream out of your teeth." Secretly a Waker:
the unlabelled jar under her counter is Waking Salts, and she has sold the
Order's secrets to exactly as many delvers as have asked the right question.

**Brannoc Two-Thumbs** — *The Cinder Bench.* Born with two thumbs on his
right hand; says it makes him twice as handy and half as welcome at weddings.
Went down to floor 38 once, came back with a brass figure the size of a thumb
stamped *No. 1*, and has never gone down again. Won't sell it. Doesn't believe
in God; believes in tolerances. Treats every item as if it has feelings,
because "things remember being made."

**Mistress Vey** — *The Hollow Coin.* Keeps a book of odds on every delver in
Kneel, updated nightly, which she will show you for a price and your own
entry for double. Her dice of the Well have eleven faces; the eleventh is
blank, and she says it's "for the house". Leans Keeper, because uncertainty
is her living and the end of the world would be terrible for business.

**Pell** — *Pell & Needle.* Never speaks above a murmur. Measures you for a
robe and a shroud in the same sitting ("saves you a trip"). Has a pincushion
shaped like a human head, eyes closed, which Pell says is "the Giant's head,
as I imagine it". Nobody in Kneel has seen the Giant's head. Pell says, "I
guessed."

**Old Hask** — *The Lending House.* One eye, one good knee, an opinion on
every buckle. Lends Kits: whole loadouts of weighed "light gear", each bound
to Hask by a candle in the back room. When a borrower dies, the kit walks
home — Hask finds it folded on the counter in the morning, damp. Hask's
ledger goes back further than Hask could possibly. Several borrowers' names
match names in the strata.

**The Chandler** — keeper of the Chandlery and head of the Candle Order in
Kneel. Gives up their name on taking office. Wears gloves of wax, remade
every morning. The only person permitted to pinch a wick. Officially of the
Hush. Keeps two candles on the desk: one lit, one not. Is sad in a way that
is almost funny, and knows it.

**Tibb** — about eight. Counts everything: delvers, steps, stars, candles,
breaths. Her numbers never match anyone else's and are always right.

**Dunstan "Dunny" Rook** — drunk ex-delver at the Stump. Made floor 34, came
back with seven fingers and a story about an automaton stamped *No. 4,112*
that "looked at me like it wanted marking." Buys nobody a drink; accepts
anyone's. There is a Rook in the Red Garden, and Dunny has never been that
deep.

**Absalom Quell** — keeper of the Little Well. Writes down everything the
well whispers in a ledger he has kept for forty-one years. Most of it is the
last words of delvers. Some of it is questions.

Background faces (for barks, notes and set dressing): **Merrow**, who keeps
the Stump and salts everything; **Jory Fenwright**, a young Waker who
chalks the next word on walls and runs; **Brother Tallis**, an Order novice
who cries when he pinches wicks; **the Widow Ferrow**, who sweeps the
Memorial Wall and corrects the spelling.

### 2.8 The Candle Order and the schism

The Order's first rule is carved over the Chandlery door: **A LIGHT FOR EACH
WHO GOES DOWN.** A second rule was carved underneath in smaller letters, and
has been scratched out so thoroughly the stone is dished.

The Order has always known more than it says. Its senior members know the
Colossus is not a statue. They know the Well is a dream. They know the strata
are peoples. What they disagree about is what to do.

**Bell Night.** Sixty-odd years ago, the Chandler of the day — Wenna Quell,
Absalom's great-aunt — climbed the bell tower at midnight and rang the bell,
"to test a theory". Every candle in the Chandlery leaned toward the pit and
stayed leaning till dawn. In the morning the Giant's left little finger was
curled a thumb's width further than the Chandlery's drawing. Wenna was never
seen again; her candle is still lit. The Order split within the week.

**The Keepers of the Hush** (grey habits; the sign is a finger laid across
the lips). They believe:
* It knelt because it was tired. You let the tired sleep.
* The ten peoples exist only while it dreams them. Waking it would be the last
  burial of ten villages.
* A god that wakes stands up. Kneel lives in its shadow, beneath its cupped
  hands. "And what do you suppose it's holding?"
* A dreamer with something new to dream about doesn't wake. Delvers are
  lullabies: they give the dream something to chew on.
The Keepers took the clapper out of the bell and dropped it down the Little
Well. They keep the candles low. They sell Hush-cloth to muffle boots.

**The Wakers** (a twist of red thread at the wrist; the sign is two knuckles
rapped on wood — a knock). They believe:
* The dream has swallowed ten villages, one after the other. Kneel is next:
  the fog closes, the cellars break through, the names repeat, the children
  count to eleven.
* You do not live in someone's dream because it's comfortable. It is theft.
* God knelt down to ask something. Someone should go all the way down and
  let it.
The Wakers make the **Waking Bells**: every one is cast with a filing from
the lip of the great bell, which is why they work, why they are expensive,
and why the lip is bright. Mother Sallow supplies the salts. Brannoc does the
casting and says he does it for the money.

**The long game.** Both sides need delvers. The Keepers want them deep enough
to feed the dream and no deeper. The Wakers want one to reach the bottom.
Neither side tells delvers this. Both have been known to lean on a candle.
The Chandler's own position is the two candles on the desk.

## 3. The Strata

Each entry: the people it dreams · what it's like · why its creatures exist ·
resources · warden · helpers · things level generation can place. Creature
stats, attacks and interactions are in [BESTIARY.md](BESTIARY.md).

### 3.1 The Undercroft — floors 1–10 — *Bowe*

**Question:** *Where did you think you were going?*

**The people.** Bowe was a village of gravediggers by conviction. They knew
God had gone down into the earth, so they reasoned that the dead go down to
God, and the deeper you lie the closer you get. At first families paid for
deeper graves. Then they dug them themselves, on Sundays, in shifts. Then the
living moved down to be near their dead, and lived in the crypts, and kept
candles for them — candles rendered from the dead's own fat, in the
great vats, "so they can light their own way." Every bone in Bowe was
washed, counted, labelled and shelved by kind: femurs with femurs, skulls
with skulls, each skull with its name painted on the brow. In the end the
whole village went down the stair together, singing, and asked the sexton to
brick the door behind them.

*Their dignity:* tenderness. No one in Bowe was ever forgotten, misfiled or
left without a light. *Their mistake:* they made death into a stair, and
then they started to hurry.

**What it's like.** Low vaults of yellow limestone, sweating. Ossuary walls
of stacked bone with painted skulls set in patterns — hearts, stars, a very
bad horse. Candle smoke and mutton fat, damp earth, bone dust that gets in
the teeth. Drips. A shovel, somewhere, always, going *chk… chk…*. The air
moves downward: every flame leans toward the next stair.

**Why its creatures exist.** The dream remembers Bowe's dead as they
understood themselves: still going down. **Ossuary shamblers** are the dream
trying to put people back together from shelves sorted by kind — forty men's
ribs in one chest. **Candle wights** are the mourners who kept the vigil
lamps and are still keeping them. **Vigil archers** were Bowe's stair-watch:
their job was to shoot anything coming *up*. **Keeners** are the professional
mourners Bowe hired for every funeral, still wailing, now for everyone.
**Grave rats**, **tallow ghouls** and **bloat flies** are the dream's memory
of what actually got into the crypts — the dream is honest about that part.

**Resources.**
* **Grave Tallow** — rendered in Bowe's vats from the dead, to light their
  own way. It remembers having been somebody, and enchantments stick to it the
  way names stick to the dead. *Crafting:* binds every enchantment; required
  by every imbue and reforge at every tier. This is why a floor-80 delver
  still needs the Undercroft.
* **Ossuary Bone** — counted, labelled, shelved. Bone keeps count.
  *Crafting:* the base of item upgrades (+1, +2 …) — each upgrade is a mark on
  a bone.

**Warden — Hollis Crane, the Sexton of Bowe** (`sexton`). Somebody had to
stay above to brick the door and keep the stair candles lit. Hollis was the
sexton; he said he'd do it. He bricked the door with his own good mortar and
kept the candles for thirty-one years, alone in an empty village, and then he
went down with his shovel to be let in. His own brickwork would not give. He
has been digging ever since, and burying anything that stands still, because
if he buries enough things surely one of them will be on the other side of
the door. He is huge because the dream remembers him the way Bowe's children
did. When he falls, he asks the delver which side of the door they're on.
Beyond his arena: the bricked door, and behind it the whole village, seated
in rows, holding hands, very small.

**Helpers.**
* **Lychdog** — Bowe buried dogs with their masters to lead them down. The
  dream's dogs still lead: a pale, silent hound that trots ahead, stops and
  stares at hidden traps, and paws at the floor above buried Reliquaries.
  Wags at candles.
* **Mercy candle** — a fat candle on a bone saucer, left by the wights for
  "whoever comes". Standing in its light heals slowly. Water or a keener's
  wail puts it out.

**Place in the level.**
* A painted skull with the name repainted three times in three hands:
  *AGNES*, *AGNESS*, *AGGIE*.
* A family niche with a pair of child's shoes on the ledge, laces tied
  together so they won't be lost.
* A bricked archway with fingernail grooves in the mortar — on *this* side.
* A pillar with depth tallies: *MAM 14 FT. DA 19 FT. ME ___*.
* A stair lined with candle stubs, each one shorter than the last, going down.
* A rendering vat with a ladle in it and a card nailed to the rim: *for Aunt
  Wenna — to see by*.
* A shovel standing upright in fresh-turned earth. The handle is warm.

### 3.2 The Drowned Archive — floors 11–20 — *Stoop*

**Question:** *What is my name?*

**The people.** Stoop was a town of copyists. They believed God had a true
name, and that a god correctly named would have to answer. The name was
long — far too long for one life — so it was divided: every child of Stoop
was given a desk at seven and a portion of the Name to write, and wrote it
until they died, and passed the desk on. They dug their reading rooms deep
under the rim for quiet, because the sound of the living spoiled the
letters. Their ink came from cuttles they farmed in the cisterns; their
vellum from goats on the rim. Proctors walked the aisles enforcing silence.

Then they struck water. It came up an inch a year. The scribes raised their
desks on stilts, then on stacked books, then built balconies, then worked
from rafts — because to stop was to lose your place, and your place was a
piece of God. When at last the portions were gathered to be read together,
the ink had run. Some notes say the cisterns burst. Some say they struck the
Well's own water. Some say it was God crying, and those notes are underlined
by someone else with *SENTIMENTAL* in the margin.

*Their dignity:* collaboration and patience. Every scribe left notes in the
margin for the next — "your hand is steadier than mine", "don't fret over
the long S", "there's a biscuit in the drawer". *Their mistake:* they thought
a name was a thing you could own; and no one ever read the whole.

**What it's like.** Flooded libraries, reading galleries half under black
water, rolling ladders, brass lamps, balconies strung with drying pages like
laundry. Wet paper, mildew, iron-gall ink, cold stone. The slap of water on
shelving, pages turning by themselves, a distant brass bell for closing time
that never finishes ringing. Lamp-jellies drifting under the surface like
reading lights someone dropped.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Drowned scribes** are Stoop, still at work,
walking along the bottom with their pens. **Proctors** still enforce
silence, now blind and very sure of it. **Folio swarms** are loose pages
that learned to flock. **Ink cuttles** and **lamp jellies** are Stoop's
livestock gone wild. **Vellum grubs** are what got into the stacks, and they
eat the notes you came to read.

**Resources.**
* **Drowned Vellum** — the only paper that doesn't care if it's wet; it has
  been wet for a thousand years. *Crafting:* reforging — rewriting an item's
  affixes needs something to write on.
* **Ink Sac** — from the cuttles; the ink still tries to spell something.
  *Crafting:* "drowned ink writes every scroll" — consumable crafting
  (scrolls, map-inks, flask labels that make flasks throw true); storm and
  void imbues.

**Warden — Magister Oddny Quell, Keeper of the Last Line** (`oddny_quell`).
Oddny was given the last line of the Name. Everyone else's portion depended
on hers: if the ending was wrong, every desk in Stoop had been wasted. So she
redrafted. And redrafted. The water rose past her knees, her waist, her
collar, and the town waited for her to finish, and drowned waiting. She
fights from a floating lectern with a pen that strikes things out. When the
page in front of her is damaged she stops fighting to correct it, which is
her tell and her tragedy. Her last line, found after the fight, is a single
word crossed out so many times the vellum has worn through.

**Helpers.**
* **Margin ghost** — the kindest copyist in Stoop, still annotating.
  Drifts near hidden notes and traps and draws a chalk ring around them; if
  you stand still near it, it writes you a helpful remark on the wall.
* **Ferry copyist** — a scribe poling a raft of lashed book-covers across the
  deep rooms. Stand on the raft and it carries you, stopping to reshelve
  anything it passes. Charges one page of vellum.

**Place in the level.**
* A desk half-submerged, a candle still burning above the waterline, a letter
  half-written.
* A ladder with a tidemark on every rung and a set of initials carved at each
  level, the handwriting getting shakier.
* Books stacked into a staircase toward the ceiling; a chair balanced on top.
* A long ribbon of paper running through three rooms — one word, unfinished.
* A QUIET PLEASE sign with a single claw-mark through QUIET.
* A drawer of pressed flowers, each labelled *evidence of outside*.

### 3.3 The Mycelial Choir — floors 21–30 — *Lean*

**Question:** *Which of you is speaking?*

**The people.** Lean believed God had not left, only gone far, and was hard
of hearing, and that no single voice could carry that far. So they sang
together. Then they learned to breathe together, in shifts, so the singing
never broke. Then someone found the pale mould in the bread cellars that
grew threads between sleepers who lay close, and in the mornings they woke
knowing each other's dreams. They lay closer. Within three generations Lean
was one voice, very loud, and very happy, and had nothing to say but *us*.

The last separate person in Lean was a woman named Ysolde, who agreed to go
in last to make sure everyone had gone in safely.

*Their dignity:* nobody in Lean was ever alone, or left behind, or sang out
of tune; the sick were carried by the song. *Their mistake:* one voice can't
hear an answer. They became too loud to listen.

**What it's like.** Caverns of stalked caps as tall as houses, gills glowing
blue-green, walls that flex very slightly as if breathing. A constant chord
that shifts key when you are noticed. Spore haze thick enough to see light
through in shafts. Smell of wet bread, turned earth, sweet rot, and, faintly,
someone's hair. Footsteps are soft; everything is soft.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Choir husks** are Lean — singers overgrown,
still singing. **Cantors** keep them in time. **Mouthpieces** are the
enormous fungal bells Lean grew to throw its voice. **Sporebacks** and **lumen
grazers** are the animals of the rim that the mould took along; they eat the
Choir as much as it eats them. **Hyphae lurkers** are the mould's own
appetite, lying in wait under the floor. **Dissonants** are the ones who
began to hear themselves again, and couldn't bear it, and couldn't get out.

**Resources.**
* **Choir Spore** — a pinch of the chord. It makes things agree with each
  other. *Crafting:* linking — putting a second effect "in harmony" with an
  item's first (imbue a second element; bind an amplifier to a focus without
  losing an affix).
* **Lumen Cap** — the blue-glowing cap that Lean ate so they could see each
  other in the dark. *Crafting:* potions, light radius, lantern cosmetics;
  every healing draught above floor 20 has lumen in it.

**Warden — Sister Ysolde Sallow, Half-Sung** (`ysolde_half_sung`). She went
in last. At the threshold of the chord she hesitated — she wanted, for one
more breath, to hear her own voice — and the chord took her anyway, halfway.
She is the only thing in the Choir that is still two things: herself, and all
of them. She speaks in two voices that argue. In the first phase the Choir
defends her as its own. In the second she tears loose, and the Choir turns
on her as well as you, and for a while the three of you fight it out.

**Helpers.**
* **Hermit cap** — a broad, slow mushroom-person who left the chord so long
  ago it no longer notices him. Sells healing caps and lumen tea from a stall
  made of his own shelf-fungus. Speaks one word at a time, carefully, like
  someone carrying water.
* **Tuning stone** — a shrine of cracked quartz. Strike it (interact or any
  force spell) and it rings a note so wrong that every choir creature within
  fifteen metres stops singing and stands stunned. One use per floor.

**Place in the level.**
* A circle of chairs with one enormous fungus grown up through all of them;
  one chair empty and clean.
* Rows of shoes at a cave mouth, each filled with soil, each sprouting a
  single cap.
* A hymnal where the four parts slowly merge onto one stave by the last page.
* A wall of handprints that turn, left to right, into gill patterns.
* A cradle with a mushroom in it. It is humming.
* *I CAN STILL HEAR MYSELF* scratched over and over; the last dozen in
  different hands.

### 3.4 The Cinder Foundry — floors 31–40 — *Raise*

**Question:** *Is it heavy?*

**The people.** Raise thought the trouble with God was size: too big to
reach, too far to hold. So they would make one small enough to carry. Every
household gave metal — wedding rings, cradle rails, grave-nails, the good
spoons. They dug **ember cores** out of the deep rock under the rim, stones
that stayed warm for years, and built forges around them. Every god they
made was an **Attempt**, numbered and logged, and every Attempt was carried
before the **Inspectors**, who were Raise's priests, and who looked at it for
a long time and stamped it *REJECT*, and it went to the slag.

Attempt No. 1 was the size of a thumb. Later ones were bigger, on the theory
that God was simply more than they'd been allowing for. The last ones were
the size of barns. The slag heap outgrew the town. Near the end, the
Inspectors began stamping the smiths.

*Their dignity:* craft. They kept beautiful logbooks. They never blamed the
metal. ("The metal is doing its best.") *Their mistake:* a god you can hold
is a tool, and they judged everything — even themselves — against a thing
they had never seen.

**What it's like.** Heat shimmer, orange light, coal dust on every surface,
the smell of sulphur and hot iron and scorched hair. Conveyors clatter
nowhere. Lava channels run in stone troughs. Quench tanks hiss. The dream
remembers the hammers in three-four time, so the whole stratum sounds like a
very slow waltz played on anvils. Walls of stamped faces. Cranes.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Attempts** are the rejected gods, still
working, still walking to the inspection stations to be judged again.
**Inspectors** still judge — and throw the rejects in the slag. **Stokers**
feed the forges. **Slag crawlers** rise from the heap where the rejects were
melted: every one of them was an Attempt, once. **Cinderlings** are sparks
that remember being fire. **Clinker mites** are the rust of the place, and
they eat the gods.

**Resources.**
* **Slag Iron** — the melted-down rejects. Every ingot was a god that wasn't
  good enough, and it has a chip on its shoulder about it. *Crafting:* heavy
  upgrades (+5 and beyond), armour, the bodies of relics.
* **Ember Core** — the warm stones Raise dug from under the rim. Nobody in
  Raise asked why the rock under the rim was warm. *(It's the dreamer's body
  heat.)* *Crafting:* fire imbues, relic charges, and the heart of anything
  that needs to keep going.

**Warden — The Last Attempt, and Idony Fenwright who is its heart**
(`last_attempt`). When the ember cores ran out, the forges went cold, and the
Last Attempt — the biggest, made of every ring and spoon Raise had left —
stood unfinished with an empty crucible in its chest. Idony Fenwright,
master of the forge, climbed in to be the warm thing inside it. It walks.
It has been waiting ever since for an Inspector to look at it and not reach
for the stamp. Its hands are shaped to hold something small, cupped
together, and are empty. In its second phase the chest opens and you can see
her, curled around the place where something small should be. Her hand is
closed around nothing. (Brannoc has No. 1. He found it in her apron on her workbench on
floor 38. He has never told anyone where it came from.)

**Helpers.**
* **Attempt No. 77** — an Attempt that decided it was a janitor. Sweeps oil,
  stamps out fires, drags cinderlings off by their tails, and fights clinker
  mites with a broom. Follows a delver who keeps things tidy.
* **Quench trough** — a stone trough of blessed water. Wading in removes
  burning, cools weapons (next fire spell deals frost too), and makes slag
  crawlers keep their distance.

**Place in the level.**
* A wall of Attempt faceplates nailed up in rows, each stamped REJECT; one
  stamped with a question mark.
* A workbench with a magnifying glass and a thumb-sized empty cradle of felt.
* A crucible full of wedding rings, unmelted, with a chalk note: *WAIT*.
* Cot rails welded into a staircase.
* A logbook open on a lectern: *Attempt 9,044. Close. Too warm.*
* A conveyor carrying a single child's shoe round and round.

### 3.5 The Gilded Hive — floors 41–50 — *Bend*

**Question:** *What do you want?*

**The people.** Bend believed God shows itself to the humble, and the
humblest thing is to serve. So they looked for the one person among them who
asked for nothing, and found Maud Ellery, who had been born unable to speak
or walk, and who had never asked for anything because she couldn't. They made
her Queen. They fed her the best of every harvest, built her a cathedral of
wax, kept bees in the rafters to sweeten her bread, and served, and served,
and served. The dream remembers them more bee than person: workers with
wax-plated backs and gauze wings grown out of habit, speaking in hums.

*Their dignity:* no one in Bend was ever hungry; everyone was fed before
themselves. *Their mistake:* they mistook silence for divinity. They never
asked her what she wanted. God — sick of being asked for things — may have
loved Bend best of all, and the dream here is sweet, and warm, and golden,
and very sad.

**What it's like.** A cathedral of hexagons, amber windows lit from behind,
gold light that makes everything look like a memory. A deep hum felt in the
teeth. Floors tacky underfoot; honey sheeting down the walls; heat. Smell of
wax, pollen, warm bread, and something sweet that has gone slightly over.
Petition boxes everywhere, all full.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Drone servants** are Bend, still carrying
food to the Queen. **Stinger guards** defend her. **Honeypot bearers** are
living larders. **Wax sealers** close up anything that leaks or intrudes.
**Larval cradles** are the nurseries — Bend kept having children and giving
them to the Hive. **Raider wasps** are wild, from outside, and after the
larvae; the dream lets them in because Bend never had walls, only doors
that opened.

**Resources.**
* **Royal Wax** — the wax of the Queen's cell, sealed by a thousand careful
  mouths. *Crafting:* sealing — lock one affix so a reforge can't touch it.
  Smeared on the skin, the Hive takes you for one of its own for a while.
* **Amber** — sap and honey gone to stone with things inside. It keeps what
  it holds. *Crafting:* preservation — reforge without losing upgrades;
  "set" a legendary so its mechanic survives an imbue.

**Warden — Maud Ellery, the Queen Who Never Asked** (`maud_ellery`). Fed for
seventy years, wedged in a cell she has grown too large to leave, she does
not attack you. Her Hive does, in waves, and she weeps honey that floods the
floor. Her swollen hands sweep the walls and bring comb down on anyone below
— not at you; at the walls. When the last guard falls she turns her head,
with great effort, and points. Up. She is the only warden who can be
*freed* rather than killed: break the wax cell and she simply stops. The
dream counts it as a kill; so do the notes. The Hive, afterwards, has
nowhere to go and sits down.

**Helpers.**
* **Deserter drone** — a worker who put its tray down and couldn't pick it
  up again. Follows you and hums the right hum, so hive creatures take longer
  to notice you. Frightened of raider wasps.
* **The Beekeeper** — a veiled figure with a smoker, of no stratum anyone can
  name. Sells smoke-pots that make the Hive drowsy. Never lifts the veil.
  Pell, asked, says "Oh — I made that veil."

**Place in the level.**
* A wax cell furnished as a bedroom for a girl: a bed, a crumpled sheet, a
  pair of small spectacles, a slate with nothing written on it.
* Amber blocks with things inside: a letter, a dog asleep, a hand holding a
  spoon.
* Tally marks pressed into a wax wall, thousands of them: meals.
* A drone frozen in amber mid-bow.
* A petition box stuffed to bursting; every petition is the same three words,
  in different hands: *what do you want*.

### 3.6 The Frozen Liturgy — floors 51–60 — *Fold*

**Question:** *Are you finished?*

**The people.** Fold kept a Mass that never ended. For four hundred years
the prayer was sung in shifts — the Hours — and never once fell silent, and
the whole town was arranged around it: bakers baked for the Hours, children
were born into their shift. The vow over the altar read: WE SHALL NOT LET THE
PRAYER FALL. Then came a winter that didn't stop. The singers thinned. With
too few left to keep the Hours, the last congregation prayed one extra
prayer inside the endless one: that the hour would not end until the prayer
was done. It didn't. Time stopped for them, mid-verse. Snowflakes hang in the
nave. The candles do not burn down.

*Their dignity:* fidelity. The old sang the young's shifts when the young
were ill, and never mentioned it. *Their mistake:* a promise kept past its
reason. They are holding a note that no longer means anything, and none of
them can be the one to let it go.

**What it's like.** Blue-white nave upon nave. One organ note, held, never
changing, so constant you stop hearing it until it stops. Snow suspended in
the air that you can walk around; frozen incense smoke like carved coral;
candle flames solid as glass. Your breath is the only thing moving. Cold iron
and old incense and nothing else — there is no rot in a place where time
doesn't pass.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Frozen pilgrims** are the congregation,
stopped mid-kneel; warmth starts their time again, and they wake furious and
confused. **Rime acolytes** swing censers of cold. **Bell haulers** carried
the great bells in procession and still do. **Penitents** kept the prayer
with the whip, in case a stopped heart was the cost. **Stillness motes** are
loose bits of the stopped hour, drifting. **Vigil icons** are the painted
saints on the walls; they watch the doors. **Rime crows** are ordinary crows
that came in from the winter and found the pilgrims' eyes very easy to peck.

**Resources.**
* **Rime Crystal** — the frost that formed on the vow itself. *Crafting:*
  frost imbues; cast speed (it holds a moment in place so the next can come
  sooner).
* **Stilled Breath** — a breath someone started and never finished, corked in
  a vial. It fogs the glass from the inside. *Crafting:* durations,
  cooldowns, anything that needs to last; the Waking Bell's clapper is
  wrapped in one.

**Warden — Celebrant Aurel Vane, Who Keeps the Hour** (`aurel_vane`). He
made the extra prayer. He is the only one in Fold who knows how long it has
been — he can see the candle on the altar not burning down. He has held the
chalice aloft for longer than Kneel has existed. His arms are frozen in
place. He wants very much to stop and cannot be the one to break the vow. He
fights with the Mass itself: bells, slow-time, the congregation. In his last
phase time *restarts*, and every frozen pilgrim in the nave moves at once.
When he falls the chalice finally comes down, and he says: "Thank you. Was
that — was that the end? Did I finish?"

**Helpers.**
* **The late pilgrim** — arrived late for Mass four hundred years ago,
  apologising, and was never frozen because she wasn't inside yet. Still
  looking for a seat. Follows you; her fussing warmth grants *blessed* and
  thaws you if you freeze.
* **Warming brazier** — a coal brazier someone set down at a side door.
  Light it and stand near it to clear chilled/frozen. Frozen pilgrims nearby
  will begin to thaw too.

**Place in the level.**
* A single snowflake hanging at eye height. You can walk all the way round it.
* A candle flame frozen solid; break it off and it is cold.
* A pew with a place kept — gloves, a hymnal, a note: *back in a minute*.
* A cracked hourglass, the sand stuck mid-fall.
* Children's footprints in the frost that stop halfway across a floor.

### 3.7 The Red Garden — floors 61–70 — *Offer*

**Question:** *Is this what I look like?*

**The people.** Offer decided God had left because there was nothing in the
world fit for it to live in: no house good enough, no body. So they would
grow one. They were gardeners first and surgeons second, and they grafted
flesh the way you graft apples. Everyone gave something — a hand, a kidney, a
length of gut, the fat of a thigh — offered on the altar in the morning and
planted in the beds by noon, each gift labelled with the donor's name so
that none went unthanked. Each villager grew a **heartseed** from a sliver of
their own heart, and the seeds were planted together to grow one heart big
enough for God. The body they grew — the **Vessel** — was enormous, and
beautiful, and nobody came to wear it. Then the garden, having been fed
people for so long, went on feeding.

*Their dignity:* generosity, and a gardener's patience — nothing given was
wasted and every gift was labelled. *Their mistake:* they thought godhood was
a matter of *more*. They grew a body with no one in it.

**What it's like.** Warm, humid, close. Walls that pulse slowly. Trellises of
bone with red vines trained along them, beds of something that is nearly
lawn, fruit that looks like organs and smells like cut grass and iron. Acid
pools where the garden digests. The drip of sap. A heartbeat under
everything, too slow, like a sleeper's.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Graft gardeners** are Offer, still tending,
still grafting — onto anything, including the dead and including you.
**Sutured brutes** are early drafts of the Vessel. **Acid guts** are the
garden's digestion, walking. **Mouthvines** and **heartseed blooms** are the
beds themselves. **Blood ticks** got into the garden from outside and never
left; they'll feed on anything warm, the Garden's creatures included.

**Resources.**
* **Ichor** — the Garden's sap, clear gold with a thread of red. It closes
  wounds and does not ask whose. *Crafting:* life and regeneration affixes,
  life-on-kill, every high healing draught.
* **Heartseed** — a seed grown from someone's heart, still beating once a
  minute. *Crafting:* planted in an item, it lets the item grow with you
  (gains item level from kills, up to a cap).

**Warden — The Vessel, worn by Tamsin Rook** (`the_vessel`). Tamsin was head
gardener. When nobody came to wear the body, she couldn't bear that the
whole village had given pieces of itself for nothing — so she climbed inside
to make it move. She can make it walk. She can make it lash out. She cannot
make it *be* anything. Its face is blank and lovely and does not fit her.
When it falls, she climbs halfway out and asks you, anxiously, whether it
looked right. There is a Rook in Kneel. He has never been this deep.

**Helpers.**
* **The Old Pruner** — a stooped gardener in a straw hat with long shears.
  Cuts back growth ahead of you (clears mouthvines and flesh walls on a path
  to the Descent), mutters about the state of the beds.
* **Leech doctor** — a peddler in a rubber apron with jars of ticks. Heals
  fully in exchange for blood (a share of max health for the floor) or
  sells ichor salves for gold.

**Place in the level.**
* A garden label on a stake: *LEFT HAND — Mrs Ottery — with thanks*.
* A wheelbarrow of teeth, sorted by size.
* A garden bench with a sun hat, secateurs and a teacup of something dark.
* A trellis trained into the shape of a man, empty.
* A lullaby carved along a row of ribs.
* Seed packets in a tray, each labelled with a name and the word *heart*.

### 3.8 The Orrery — floors 71–80 — *Reach*

**Question:** *Where are you taking me?*

**The people.** Reach lived close enough to the beginning to know God was
asleep — Stand told them. Waking it seemed rude, and frightening. But a
sleeper can be *turned*, gently, the way you turn someone who is snoring. So
Reach built the Orrery: a machine of gears matched to stars and seasons,
fitted around the dreamer's sleep, so that each turn of the great wheel would
turn the dream a degree toward kinder winters, better harvests, fewer wars.
It worked, a little. Then every correction needed another correction. Then
they found the machine was turning them too — you can't steer a dream from
outside it — and by then they couldn't stop.

Reach's neighbours, Turn, put out every light in the world to hide from God,
including the stars Reach used to set its gears. Reach built angels to relight
them. The two have been at war, quietly, ever since.

*Their dignity:* ambition for others. They wanted nothing for themselves; they
published every calculation. *Their mistake:* they never asked. They decided
for the sleeper.

**What it's like.** Brass and blued steel, rooms that rotate, bridges made of
gear teeth, pendulums the size of houses sweeping through halls. Ticking at
every scale, from a watch in your ear to something vast that ticks once a
minute and shakes dust from the ceiling. Oil, ozone, cold metal, chalk.
Painted stars on the ceilings that move when you're not looking.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Clockwork angels** tend the machine and
relight the stars. **Governors** stop anything from going too fast.
**Escapement crabs** repair. **Mainspring hounds** chase down loose parts.
**Astrolabe eyes** watch the sky for Turn. **Rust moths** are wild, eat oil,
and are the only thing in the Orrery that is not on schedule.

**Resources.**
* **Orichalcum Gear** — a gear cut to the period of a particular star.
  *Crafting:* cast speed and cooldowns; reforging relics.
* **Mainspring** — a coil of stored turning. *Crafting:* extra charges,
  extra jumps, relic cooldowns; the thing inside every Timed amplifier.

**Warden — Horologer Ione Castellan, the Brake** (`ione_castellan`). When
she realised the machine was turning Reach along with the dream, she tried to
stop it, and found the only way was to throw her weight on the great brake
and hold it. She has held it since. If she lets go, the Orrery spins free.
In her second phase she lets go — to fight you with both hands — and the
whole arena accelerates. When she falls she asks where they were going: "I
had it written down."

**Helpers.**
* **The Oiler** — a small robed figure with a long-spouted can. Oils stalled
  lifts and gear-bridges so they run, making shortcuts. Leaves oil everywhere,
  which is its own kind of help.
* **Unwound angel** — an angel whose spring has run down, kneeling in a
  corner. Wind it (interact, a few seconds) and it fights for you until it
  runs down again.

**Place in the level.**
* A cot bolted to a gear, rotating slowly, blanket strapped down.
* Blueprints of the Colossus with arrows: *turn 3° north — gentler winters*.
* A chalkboard of calculations ending *and then we will ask it*, with *ask*
  rubbed out and *tell* written in.
* A tea set glued to a table that turns.
* A star chart naming three villages in the margin: Stand, Turn, Offer.

### 3.9 The Unlit — floors 81–90 — *Turn*

**Question:** *Where are you?*

**The people.** After God went quiet, Turn decided to hide, the way children
hide, so the parent has to come looking. They put out every fire. They
blinded their windows, and then their eyes. They hung mirrors everywhere so
they would see if a light came. And they waited in the dark, very quietly,
counting under their breath — the counting of hide-and-seek — for someone to
come and find them.

*Their dignity:* faith that they were worth looking for. An entire people who
chose to be found rather than to demand. *Their mistake:* they hid so well
that nothing could find them — and hiding is also a way of asking.

It is looking. That is what **the Seeker** is.

**What it's like.** Total dark, apart from what you bring. Halls of mirrors,
most turned to face the wall. Your lantern comes back at you from a hundred
angles, and not every reflection moves when you do. Cold drafts. Somewhere,
always, a voice counting: *…ninety-seven, ninety-eight…* Snuffed wicks.
Stone that has never been warm.

**Why its creatures exist.** **Reflections** are Turn, living inside their
mirrors where the dark is best. **Snuffers** put lights out. **Hiders** wait.
**Echo mimics** answer back. **Lightmoths** are the only thing in the Unlit
that wants light, and they will find yours. **The Seeker** is the dream
itself, looking, enormous and blind, drawn to light and to hiding things.

**Resources.**
* **Umbral Glass** — mirror silvered with the dark. *Crafting:* stealth,
  void imbues, lenses.
* **Echo** — a sound that never finished bouncing, caught in a stoppered
  horn. *Crafting:* repetition — extra projectiles, Echoing and Twinned
  amplifiers, copying one affix onto another item.

**Warden — Olly Ferrow, Who Hid Best** (`olly_ferrow`). A child of Turn who
was better at the game than anyone. Never found. The Seeker has been looking
for her longest. She is the warden because she doesn't want to be found:
the game is all she has. She is invisible except in mirrors and in reflected
light; she moves when you look away; she throws your own light back at you.
When you finally catch her, she laughs, and says "Found me!", and then:
"Where were *you*? I waited ever so long." Somewhere in the stratum, the
Seeker stops moving. There is a Widow Ferrow who sweeps the Memorial Wall.

**Helpers.**
* **The Counter** — a ghost sitting with its face in its hands, counting.
  Within the sound of its voice you are *home*: the Seeker will not take
  you and reflections won't step out.
* **Blind guide** — an elder of Turn who knows every wall by touch. Follows
  your footsteps; if you put your light out and walk behind them, they lead
  you to the Descent.

**Place in the level.**
* A cupboard with small handprints on the inside of the door.
* Hundreds of mirrors turned to face the wall; one turned back.
* A snuffed candle with teeth marks in the wick.
* A chalk tally on a wall that ends: *99,999,998*.
* A pair of spectacles with the lenses painted black.

### 3.10 The Threshold — floors 91–100 — *Stand, and Kneel*

**Question:** withheld. See §5.

**The people.** Stand is the oldest village: the people who were at the rim
when God came to the edge of the world, knelt, set its head down into the
earth, and slept. They did not try to reach God. They watched it go. Then
they asked the first question — *where did it go?* — and everyone has been
asking ever since.

The Threshold is the dreamer's own mind, where the dream is thinnest. It
dreams of the last thing it saw — a village at the rim, people standing —
and fills it with whatever village is at the rim *now*. So it dreams Kneel.
Wrong.

**What it's like.** It is Kneel. The Market Ring has forty stalls. The houses
face the pit instead of the square. The fog is *inside* the houses, pressed
against the windows; outside is clear. There is no Colossus: the rim where it
should kneel is empty, and the pit goes down and down to a pale shape. The
villagers are there — Sallow, Brannoc, Vey, Hask — but assembled from each
other's parts, and they are all standing at the rim, staring. The Chandlery's
shelves go up out of sight: there is a candle here for everyone who ever
lived in any of the eleven villages. The air smells of every stratum at once,
faintly, like a house where many meals have been cooked.

**Why its creatures exist.** This is the dreamer's own mind, and it
remembers everything that ever came down — so it can make anything. Its
creatures are memories: **remembered villagers**, **kneelers** that move
only when unseen, **the unfinished** (drafts of creatures from every
stratum, not quite decided), **godhands** rising from the ground, **wrong
candles**, and **dream delvers** wearing the names of real fallen players.

**Resources.**
* **Godtear** — a tear the dreamer cried in its sleep. The dream keeps what
  it touches; a godtear *is* what it touched. *Crafting:* legendary work —
  raise an epic to legendary, reroll a unique's mechanic, or make the dream
  "remember" an item so it can't be lost to a Reliquary once.

**Warden (floor 100) — Anneth of Stand, the First Asker**
(`anneth_of_stand`). The first person of the first village. She watched God
kneel and set its head down, and she went down after it that same night,
before anyone, and she has been kneeling beside the Head ever since, asking
it to come back. She asked first, and most, and loudest. She will fight you
to be answered first. She has watched every people go by and fights with all
of their tricks — candles, ink, song, hammer, honey, frost, flesh, gears, the
dark. When she falls, she says: "It never answers. It never once — " and
then, looking at you properly for the first time: "Oh. You're not here to ask
it anything, are you."

**Helpers.**
* **Your candle** — somewhere on each Threshold floor burns a candle with
  your name scratched in it, in your own handwriting. Carry it (it's light,
  and it's yours) and it heals you while lit and shows the Descent as a lean
  in the flame. If you die with it in hand, your Reliquary is placed on the
  floor above instead.
* **The Witness** — a figure of Stand, standing at the edge of each floor's
  pit, watching. Stand beside it for ten seconds and it points to the one
  thing on the floor that is *not* a memory: the way down.

**Place in the level.**
* Your own stash chest, open, with your own things in it — but wrong
  colours.
* The Memorial Wall, with your name on it, and a floor number you haven't
  died on yet.
* The Stump, with every chair taken except the one nobody sits in.
* The Founders' Plinth, with a statue on it at last. It is a statue of the
  delver who is looking at it.
* A pocket stone on a doorstep, still warm from someone's pocket.

## 4. The Colossus and the Head

**The body.** A hundred metres of grey that everybody calls stone. It is what
the skin of a god becomes after kneeling in the weather for longer than there
has been weather. No tool marks. Lichen in rings. The knees sunk into the
bedrock like knees into mud. It knelt in whatever it was wearing, and the
dream-weather has turned the cloth the same grey; Kneel calls the folds
"robes". The neck ends smooth and round, like a wrist.

**The hands.** Cupped over the pit — not the way you cup water, but the way
you cup a candle flame against the wind. The Keepers' question, "what do you
suppose it's holding?", has an answer: it is sheltering the dream. The Well is
a flame it keeps out of the draught. If it wakes and stands, the hands open.

**Small facts a player can check.**
* The stone is warm at night, warmest on the palm and thumb.
* Stand still long enough on the Thumb and you'll feel a heartbeat — one every
  twenty seconds.
* The left little finger has moved once in living memory (Bell Night). Every
  time the bell is rung, it twitches.
* A stair runs up inside the robe folds behind the left knee, cut by hands
  older than Kneel's. At the top, on the neck, is a shrine: a stone cup, a
  ring of pocket stones worn smooth, and words cut around the rim of the neck
  in the oldest script in the game: *IT IS ONLY SLEEPING*. Pocket stones are
  older than Kneel.

**The Head.** At the bottom of the Godwell, face up, the size of a hill. Eyes
closed. Lips very slightly parted. Its hair is the roots of everything above.
It breathes — the Well's breath is its breath, and the tides that reshape
the floors are its sleeping lungs. On its left cheek are handprints, a few
thousand of them, all one person's (Anneth's). Each people drew the face
differently: Bowe painted it as a skull, Stoop wrote it as a word, Lean sang
it as a chord, Raise cast it as a mask of rejected faces, Bend never drew it,
Fold carved it weeping, Offer grew it, Reach charted it, Turn refused to look.
In the game it should be *plain*: lichen-grey, calm, faintly tired, a face at
the end of a very long shift, tilted a few degrees as if listening.

## 5. Floor 100 and the final question

The canon is fixed: God will ask *you* something, and the text does not say
what it means. These are build notes so floor 100 can exist without closing
the question.

**Layout.** Floor 100 is Kneel, remembered wrong, at full scale: the Market
Ring with forty stalls, the Chandlery, the Stump, the rim — and no Colossus.
Where the Godwell portal should be there is the real pit, and a spiral of
kneeling-stones going down around its wall, about forty metres, to a ledge at
the level of the Head's closed eyes (**the eyelid terrace**). Anneth of Stand
fights in the Market Ring and on the upper turns of the stair. No other
creatures on floor 100 once she falls; the remembered villagers all stand at
the rim and watch, and turn their backs as you go down (the send-off custom,
§2.4, finally explained).

**The Head doesn't fight.** It breathes. Every breath out pushes loose props
and bodies gently away from the face; every breath in draws them toward it.
Stand on the terrace long enough (and do nothing hostile) and its lips move.

**The question** appears as a single line of text, in no speaker's name, and
then the game waits. Principles:

1. It must be answerable by *doing*, not by picking from a menu.
2. There must be no correct answer, and no answer the game refuses.
3. The game must never say what the answer did.

Suggested candidate questions for the team to choose between (or rotate,
since the dream is never the same twice): *"Is it morning?"*, *"Did you come
all this way to ask me for something?"*, *"What are they like, up there,
now?"*, and a personalised one built from the delver's own record ("Why the
rats?" for someone who killed four thousand of them).

**Answers are acts** using verbs the game already has:
* **Ring a Waking Bell** — the Wakers' answer.
* **Light a candle** on the terrace (any fire spell on the ledge's candle
  stub) and sit — the Hush's answer.
* **Raise the Open Palm** (the pact sign) — offer God a pact. Nobody in any
  stratum ever thought to do this.
* **Walk away** — step back onto the stair and Ascend.
* **Attack it** — it doesn't react. The dream records it anyway.

Record each player's answer. **In Kneel, change one small physical thing per
answer**, globally, cumulatively, never explained: each Bell nudges the little
finger; each candle makes the Chandlery's flames lean further; each pact moves
the fog back by one stall; each walk-away shortens the Long Candle by a
fingernail. Tibb counts the answers and will tell anyone the totals. The
delver wakes at the Stump with bread and salt, and nobody asks them anything
until morning.

## 7. Notes for writers

**Recurring motifs** — use them, don't explain them: candles; questions;
postures; the recurring surnames (Quell, Rook, Sallow, Crane, Fenwright,
Ferrow); pocket stones; counting; hands; "the dream keeps what it touches";
turning your back; salt.

**Voices by people:**
* *Bowe* — plain, domestic, wobbly spelling, lists of names and depths.
* *Stoop* — precise, footnoted, petty in the margins, very kind in the margins.
* *Lean* — starts with "I", drifts to "we", ends with "us"; no names.
* *Raise* — logbook: numbers, tolerances, weights, trade words, grief in
  parentheses.
* *Bend* — deferential, sweet, "she", never "I want".
* *Fold* — rubrics and hours, liturgical capitals, exhausted.
* *Offer* — garden journal: seed packets, labels, weather, thanks.
* *Reach* — confident calculation, degrees and periods, "we have decided".
* *Turn* — whispers, counting, very short lines, the word *soon*.
* *Stand* — almost nothing; a few words cut in stone.
* *Kneel* — dry, practical, funny to keep from being frightened.
* *Delvers* — receipts, tallies, dares, wills, bad jokes, apologies.

**Things writers know that players shouldn't be told:**
* The Long Candle is lit for the first one who went down: God. It never gets
  shorter because the one it's lit for isn't dead. (Threshold ledger: "We lit
  one for It, because It went down first.")
* Old Hask's ledger has entries in Hask's hand for borrowers from Stoop,
  Raise, Offer and Turn. Hask is not explained. Hask is not to be explained.
* Wenna Quell (Bell Night) is not dead; her candle burns. Where she is, is
  not written. A Wenna appears in Bowe's rendering-vat card. Leave it.
* Pell's pincushion is correct.

**Never:** chosen ones, prophecies, dark lords, ancient evils, elves, dwarves,
orcs, destiny, "the prophecy speaks of". Nothing is evil here. Everyone was
trying to reach somebody.
